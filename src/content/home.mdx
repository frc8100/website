FIRST Robotics Competition, or FRC, is a global high school robotics competition where teams have only a few weeks to design, build, program, and test a robot for a completely new game every year.

For us, the robot is only part of it. We spend our time learning from mistakes, debating design ideas, helping each other figure things out, and celebrating when something finally works. The skills we gain through that process stick with us long after the competition is over.

We are Grayson Robotics Team 8100, a student-led FRC team from Grayson High School in Loganville, Georgia.

Our team brings together students with all kinds of interests and experience. Some of us spend most of our time writing code, some are designing and building mechanisms, and others work on things like outreach, fundraising, media, and business. What brings us together is that we get to build something real and figure out how to make it better.

Every season is different, and we are always learning something new along the way.

For the 2026 season, our robot is **Wooden Warrior**.

This year's FRC game, **REBUILT**, challenges alliances to collect and score Fuel while navigating the field.
Our robot is built around collecting Fuel and shooting it into the Hub, with the rest of the season spent making those systems faster, more consistent, and easier to control.

Wooden Warrior is the result of weeks of designing, building, testing, breaking things, and trying again. Every part of the robot has a story behind it and we're excited to keep improving it throughout the season.

    imageAlt="Members teaching elementary school students about engineering"
    imageSide="left"
    variant="light"

Outside of the competition season, we work to make STEM more accessible to students in our community and give younger students the opportunity to discover engineering for themselves.

Every summer, we host a week long engineering camp at our school for more than 40 elementary and middle school students. During the week, our robotics members volunteer to help students explore different areas of STEM through hands on projects and activities including programming with Scratch and designing in Autodesk Inventor and Tinkercad. We hope that by giving students an early, hands-on introduction to engineering, we can help spark an interest that lasts well beyond the week.
